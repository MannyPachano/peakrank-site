Which 5 Pages A Contractor Website Usually Needs

  • Home. What you do, where you work and how to reach you, readable on a phone in a few seconds.
  • Services. The jobs you do, with enough detail for a customer to know you handle theirs.
  • Service area. The towns and counties you cover.
  • About. Who runs the business, your license and insurance details, and photos of your crew and trucks.
  • Contact. A quote request form, your phone number and your email.

Add pages when one service brings in most of your work or gets searched on its own. A plumber might add separate pages for drain cleaning and water heaters. A roofer might add a storm damage page. How This Compares With A Website Builder Or A Monthly Website Plan

A do-it-yourself builder costs less money up front and more of your time. You pick the template, write the pages, size the photos and fix the phone layout yourself, and the monthly fee keeps running for as long as the site is up. When you stop paying, the site goes offline.

Monthly website plans from agencies bundle the design, the hosting and the updates into one fee. That's convenient, but the site often stays in the agency's account, and leaving can mean starting over. Before you sign one, check the contract for who owns the design and the domain.
